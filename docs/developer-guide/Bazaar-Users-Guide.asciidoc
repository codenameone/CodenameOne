[[bazaar-users-guide]]
== Bazaar: A commerce platform from four sides

Bazaar is a sample commerce platform with a server behind it. Shoppers browse a
store, fill a cart and check out. The merchant's staff manage the catalog and the
stock, pack and ship the orders, and sell over the counter on a register that
keeps working with no network. An administrator runs the platform the stores
live on. The app and the server are one Maven project, written in Java on both
sides.

This chapter is the user's guide: what each kind of user sees and can do.
<<bazaar-implementors-guide>> is the other half. It explains how the project is
built and what to replace before it takes real orders.
<<bazaar-feature-comparison>> sets Bazaar's features beside other commerce
platforms.

One app serves everyone, on a phone, a tablet, a desktop or in a browser. The
server gives every account a set of roles, and the app opens on the screen that
fits them. On a phone the sections are tabs at the bottom; on a wide screen they
are a sidebar, and on a desktop the app adds native menus, keyboard shortcuts,
right-click menus on rows and second windows.

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Role | What it adds

| Shopper
| Every account is a shopper, and a guest can browse and fill a cart before
  signing in. Browse and search a store, choose variants, check out, follow the
  order, keep a wishlist and saved addresses, and review what they bought.

| Owner or manager
| Runs a store: the dashboard, products, inventory, orders and fulfilment,
  customers, discounts, gift cards, shipping and taxes, analytics, automations,
  webhooks and the store's settings.

| Cashier
| Works the store's register: opens a shift, sells, takes returns and closes the
  shift with a cash count.

| Administrator
| Runs the platform: approves and suspends stores, manages accounts and sees the
  platform's payments and totals.
|===

=== Trying it

Start the server on the development profile and run the app, as
<<bazaar-launch>> shows. The development profile creates these accounts, all
with the password `bazaar-demo`:

[cols="2,3"]
|===
| Account | Who

| `shopper@bazaar.example`, `shopper2@bazaar.example`
| Shoppers

| `shopper3@bazaar.example`
| A shopper an administrator has flagged

| `owner@bazaar.example`
| Owns Northwind Outfitters

| `cashier@bazaar.example`
| Works the Northwind register; the register PIN is `1234`

| `admin@bazaar.example`
| Runs the platform
|===

It also creates two stores. Northwind Outfitters (handle `northwind`) sells
apparel and outdoor gear from a warehouse, a downtown store and a weekend pop-up.
Little Clay Studio (handle `littleclay`) sells ceramics from one studio shop. Each
has about thirty products with stock that's plentiful, low and gone, collections,
a supplier and an open purchase order, shipping zones and tax rates, and three
discount codes: `WELCOME10` (10% off a first order), `FREESHIP` (free shipping
over $75) and `BUNDLE` (buy 2, get 1 free).

Cards are simulated: `4242 4242 4242 4242` is accepted and `4000 0000 0000 0002`
is declined. No money moves.

=== The shopper

The app opens straight into shopping. Nobody has to sign in to look around: the
Discover tab shows every store on the platform, what's popular and the newest
products across all of them, and Search runs across every store. Opening a store
shows its own page: its banner, its collections and new arrivals, and a search
that stays inside the store with filters for collection, price and availability.
The arrow on the banner goes back to Discover.

A product page shows its photos, its description and reviews, and lets the
shopper choose a variant, such as a size and a color. A variant that's out of
stock says so and can't be added. Every store has a cart of its own, kept on
the server for a guest too, and the Cart tab shows the one of the store the
shopper is in, with a discount code field and an estimate of shipping and tax.

The app asks for a sign-in only where an account is needed: at checkout, to
follow a store, to save to the wishlist, and on the Orders and Account tabs. The
guest's cart moves into the account, and the checkout carries on. Checkout asks
for the address, from the saved ones or a new one, the shipping method the
store offers for it (including pickup or local delivery where the store has
them), and the card. A returning shopper finds their default address and card
already chosen, so a repeat order is one tap on Place order. The card is held,
not charged, until the order ships.

Following a store puts its new products in a row of their own at the top of
Discover. The orders tab follows each order through its steps: placed, packed,
shipped and delivered, and shows each parcel's scans from the carrier, newest
first. An order can be cancelled until it's packed. The shopper can download the
invoice as a PDF, ask to return lines of a delivered order, and review the
products they bought.

The account tab holds the profile, saved addresses, the wallet of cards and the
settings. Store staff and administrators sign in from the account tab too; their
role decides which home they land on.

=== The merchant

==== Dashboard and analytics

The dashboard shows today's sales, orders to fulfil, low stock and recent
activity. Analytics charts sales over a chosen period, by channel (online or
register), top products, the checkout funnel and customer segments, and exports
what it shows as CSV.

==== Products and collections

Products are a table on a wide screen and a list on a phone. A product has a
title, description, type, vendor, tags, photos and options; the variant matrix
generates one variant per combination of options, each with its own price, SKU,
barcode and weight. Photos can be dropped onto the editor from the desktop.
Products can be duplicated, edited in bulk, and imported or exported as CSV.
A product can be sold as a bundle: pick its component variants and how many of
each go into one, and give the bundle its own price. Selling a bundle takes its
components out of stock, and the storefront lists what it includes. A product
can also be scheduled per channel (the online store, the register and each
marketplace), with times to publish and unpublish it, in UTC.
A variant marked as a digital download ships nothing: upload its file, set how
many times and for how many days a buyer's link works, and paste licence keys
if it needs them. A cart of downloads only checks out without an address or a
delivery option. Once the order is paid, the links (and a key per unit) appear
in the order mail, on the confirmation page and on the buyer's order page; a
register sale emails them. A full refund stops a line's link, and the order page
in the back office can revoke one by hand. The gallery also takes videos (an
MP4 or WebM file, or an `https` link) and 3D models (`.glb` files), in any order
with the pictures; the storefront plays the video and shows the model in a
viewer you can turn and zoom, and the app links the model file.

A product can have a category from Google's product taxonomy, found by typing a
word of it ("tea", "shoes") in the product editor. Shopping feeds send it, and a
collection page in the browser offers its products' categories as a filter.
Each variant can also carry customs details, an HS code and a country of origin,
which go to the carrier for a parcel that leaves the country and onto the
packing slip of such an order; and a measure with the unit to price it by (250
g, priced per 100 g), so shoppers see "$4.00 / 100 g" beside the price.
Collections are either chosen by hand or smart, defined by rules such as a tag or
a price range.

==== Inventory

The inventory screen shows what's on hand, committed and available for each
variant at each location. Stock can be adjusted with a reason, counted, moved
between locations with a transfer, and received against a purchase order sent to
a supplier. A barcode scanner, the camera or a keyboard scanner on a desktop,
finds a variant by its code. Every change is listed in the variant's history.

A variant can keep selling when it runs out; the shortfall shows as backordered
on the order. Stock that is on hand but cannot be sold is set aside as
unavailable with a reason: damaged, in quality control or held as safety stock.
Variants can be tracked by serial number or by lot with an expiry date, and
lots are picked first-expiring-first. Barcode labels print as a PDF, on Avery
30-up sheets or 2x1 inch thermal labels, for chosen variants or for everything
a purchase order brought in.

==== Orders and fulfilment

The orders list filters by status, channel and payment. An order shows its lines,
payments, notes and timeline, and can be cancelled, refunded in part or in full,
or marked paid. A draft order is an order the merchant builds by hand, for a phone
sale; a pay link emails the buyer a checkout page for it, and the link is printed
on the invoice. Orders can be archived out of the list, by hand or automatically
once fulfilled and paid, and many orders can be fulfilled, labelled, printed,
marked paid, archived or tagged at once.

The store's return rules set the return window, which products and collections
are final sale, a restocking fee and a return shipping fee. A shopper asking for
a return chooses a refund, store credit or an exchange for another variant; an
exchange that costs more sends a pay link for the difference. Every email and the
register's SMS receipt can be reworded per language, previewed and reset.

The fulfilment board shows every open order as a card in To pack, Packed, Shipped
or Delivered. Dragging a card to the next column packs it, buys a label and ships
it, or marks it delivered; on a phone each card has a button that moves it on.
The board makes a pick list for a column as a PDF, and opens the returns queue,
where returned goods are received and refunded. Packing slips, labels, invoices,
credit notes and purchase orders can be previewed, printed, shared or, on a
desktop, dragged out of the window as files.

==== Customers, discounts and gift cards

The customers screen lists the store's customers with their orders, total spent,
tags and store credit. Discounts are codes for a percentage or an amount off,
free shipping, or buy some and get some free, with limits on use and dates. Gift
cards are issued with a code and a QR code, and their balance can be checked.

==== Shipping, taxes and settings

Shipping zones group countries, each with rates: flat, by weight, by price, free
over an amount, pickup or local delivery. Tax rates are set per country and
region. The settings hold the store's name, description, logo and banner,
locations, staff and their roles, and the registers.

*Checkout settings*, in the back office on the web, choose the payment methods
the store takes beside cards (Klarna, Afterpay/Clearpay, iDEAL, Bancontact,
SEPA debit and more). Checkout offers each one only where the order's currency
and country allow it. An order paid by a pay-later or bank method shows
"payment pending" until the payment clears; if it fails, the order is
cancelled and its stock goes back on sale. The same page turns on tips, with
up to four presets such as `15%, 20%, 5.00`, and lists the questions shoppers
answer at checkout, one per line as `Label | text, checkbox or select |
required | choice; choice`. Tips are charged outside the order total and show in
the Tips report; answers show on the order, in the confirmation mail and in the
app. Shoppers with Apple Pay or Google Pay see a wallet button on the product
page, the cart and the checkout. The app pays by card and offers *Pay another
way*, which opens the store's web checkout.

==== Automations

An automation is drawn as a graph: a trigger, such as an order being paid or
stock running low, leads through conditions, such as an order total over an
amount or a customer tag, to actions, such as tagging the order, notifying staff,
emailing the customer, creating a discount code, drafting a reorder, sending a
webhook or waiting. Nodes are added from the palette and joined by dragging from
an output to an input. The store starts with two examples. An automation is saved
as a draft until it's switched on, and its activity log shows what each run did.

==== Webhooks

The webhooks page registers addresses to be told about orders, refunds, low stock,
products and customers. Each webhook has a secret that signs its deliveries, and
the page lists every delivery with its status and response and can send one again.

=== The register

A cashier signs in, chooses the register and enters the PIN (`1234` for the demo
cashier), and opens a shift with the cash in the drawer. The sell screen is a
grid of products and a cart; scanning a barcode adds the item. A sale can be
attached to a customer, discounted, and paid with any mix of card, cash, gift card
and store credit, with change worked out for cash. The receipt can be printed,
emailed or shown as a QR code, and a second window on a desktop shows the cart to
the customer.

The register keeps selling with no network. Sales wait in the outbox and are sent
when the connection comes back; the outbox screen shows each one's state, and a
sale the server refused, such as one paid with a gift card that ran out while the
register was offline, can be retried after it's sorted out. Returns are taken
against a receipt. At the end of the shift the cashier counts the drawer, and the
shift report shows the expected and counted cash.

The register works with the counter's hardware. Under Devices, a manager pairs a
card reader by typing the code it shows (`simulated-front` pairs the demo's
simulated reader); with a reader at the register, Card sends the amount to it and
the sale waits for the tap, and without one the standalone terminal is used. The
same screen takes the network address of an ESC/POS receipt printer
(`192.168.1.50` or `192.168.1.50:9100`): the receipt then prints on it, and a cash
sale opens the drawer wired to the printer.

Pickups lists the online orders the shopper chose to collect at this register's
location. Ready for pickup tells the shopper by e-mail and notification, and Picked
up hands the order over. Ship to customer sends the sale on the register to an
address instead: it quotes the store's delivery options with the total each makes,
takes payment, and the order is packed and shipped from this location like an
online order. In the back office, Commissions sets each staff member's rate and
reports what they sold at the counter in the last 30 days; a sale keeps the rate of
the day it was made.

=== The administrator

The platform console shows the number of stores, orders and the total sold across
the platform. The stores screen approves pending stores and suspends or reinstates
active ones, with a reason that stays in the store's history. The users screen
finds accounts, grants and removes roles, and flags or blocks an account. The
payments screen lists payments and payouts across the stores.

=== In a browser

The server can host the app itself, so the whole platform runs in a browser with
nothing installed; `backend/server.sh run --web` builds it and serves it at
`http://localhost:8080/`. Beside the app, the same server serves a complete web
version of every side of the platform as ordinary HTML pages, which work without
JavaScript and load quickly on any device. The rest of this chapter describes
those pages; most of what they do is also on the app's screens.

[[bazaar-web-shop]]
==== Shopping on the web

Each store lives at `/s/<handle>`: home, collections, search, product pages, the
store's own pages and blog, and a cart. The header has a globe menu for the
language (English, Spanish, French, German and Hebrew, each named in its own
language) and, when the store sells in more than one market, a market menu
beside it. A shopper who picks a market sees prices in that market's currency
and, when the store has translated them, product names and descriptions in its
language. Both menus work from the keyboard, and without JavaScript they are
plain forms. The header's *Open the app* link goes to the Bazaar app at `/app/`,
and the platform's home page carries a card for it.

Checkout happens on the page. A guest gives an email address and a delivery
address; a signed-in shopper picks one of the saved addresses. Delivery options
refresh as the address changes, discount and gift card codes are applied in
place, and the card is held when the order is placed. A shopper who chose a
market is charged in that market's currency, at the rate the page showed. The
confirmation page and its invoice open from a link in the confirmation email,
so a guest can come back to them without an account.

A product that's out of stock but on pre-order can still be bought; the cart
and the confirmation say the item ships when stock arrives. An item that isn't
on pre-order offers a form to be emailed when it's back. A shopper who left a
cart gets a reminder whose link restores that cart on any device.

The chat bubble in the corner of every store page opens a conversation with
the store. It shows whether anyone on the staff is online, when they're typing,
and a tick for each message once it is delivered and another once it is read.
The conversation follows the shopper from page to page and, once they sign in,
to their account on another device. A shopper who leaves an email address gets
replies there when the chat is closed. While someone on the staff is online the
shopper can also place a voice or video call from the chat. The cookie banner asks once which optional cookies the
shopper accepts, and the privacy page changes the answer later.

A visitor can create an account at `/signup` and sign in with a password. Once
signed in, `/account` holds:

* orders, with tracking, invoices and a return form once an order is delivered;
* saved addresses, the wishlist and the reviews they wrote;
* loyalty points, the member card as a QR code, and clubs they belong to;
* back-in-stock alerts and, per store, whether they receive marketing;
* subscriptions, which can be paused, resumed, skipped or cancelled;
* security: an authenticator app for two-step sign-in, recovery codes, and
  signing out every other session.

[[bazaar-web-b2b]]
==== Buying for a company

A buyer the merchant has added to a company sees the company's prices in the
store and a company section in the account. From there the buyer orders by SKU
on a quick-order form or by uploading a CSV file of SKUs and quantities, asks
for a quote, accepts or declines a quote the store sent, and downloads the
invoices of orders placed on net terms. Quantities follow the price list's
rules, such as a minimum or cases of six.

[[bazaar-web-back-office]]
==== The back office on the web

A merchant who signs in at `/auth/signin` reaches the back office at
`/admin/<store>`. Staff whose account has two-step sign-in enrolled enter their
authenticator code too. The dashboard shows the day's sales and charts; the
sections below are reached from the menu.

*Orders and stock.* Orders can be searched, refunded or cancelled, and fulfilled
from the order page: pack a parcel, buy a label at one of the carrier's rates,
then mark it shipped, with the packing slip and label as PDFs. The fulfilment
board moves orders a column at a time. Products, inventory and customers have
tables that sort, filter and export to CSV.

*Warehouse.* The warehouse board of a location shows purchase orders arriving
(receiving, put away) and orders leaving (pick, pack, dispatch), with the bins and
a pick list that walks them in order. Each transfer and purchase order has a
timeline of when it was sent and received. The forecast page shows how long
each item's stock will last at each location at its recent rate of sale, and
drafts the purchase orders and transfers that would cover the shortfall.

*Selling.* Discounts and sales have editors; a sale changes prices when it starts
and puts them back when it ends. The promotions calendar shows a month of sales,
campaigns and launches, and an event can be moved to another day. Subscriptions
are set up as selling plans on a product, with renewals and failed payments
listed. Companies hold sites, buyers, a price list and payment terms; quotes
move across a board from draft to ordered, and unpaid net-terms invoices can be
marked paid.

*Marketing.* A segment is built from rules (spent more than, ordered in the last,
lives in) with a live count of who matches. A campaign sends an email or text to
a segment, with a test send to yourself first and an optional A/B test of two
subjects. Journeys are flowcharts that walk each customer through waits,
messages and branches, with the number of customers at each step. The loyalty
page sets how points are earned and the tiers; clubs are paid memberships that
unlock member prices, members-only products and early access. Signup forms collect
email addresses, and only people who agreed receive marketing.

*Markets and tax.* A market groups countries under a currency and language, with
an exchange rate the merchant enters, a price adjustment, rounding and, where
wanted, fixed prices. The translations page edits product, collection and page
text per language, each field beside its original and marked missing, outdated
(the original changed since) or done. A product's sheet covers its title,
description, SEO text, option names and values, pictures' alternative text and
text metafields; a product's page links to it, and the app's product editor has
a Translations card. A whole catalog goes out to a translator as one CSV per
language and comes back the same way. Shoppers see the translation for the
language they chose, else their market's, else their browser's, on every page,
in the cart and checkout, and in order mail. Products can carry customs codes, and duty rates per
destination give the shopper an estimate of import duties at checkout. The tax
page reports tax collected by region for a period, as a CSV for filing, and
records customers' tax exemption certificates.

*Risk.* Each online order is scored for fraud. A high score holds the order:
it can't be fulfilled until a manager approves or cancels it from the risk page.
Payment disputes reported by the processor appear with their deadline; the
merchant attaches evidence (the order's own details are one click), submits it,
or accepts the dispute.

*Online store.* The theme page picks colors, font, corner style and logo, shows
the result on a preview link that can be shared for an hour, and publishes it.
Pages and policies, blog posts, the header and footer menus, and redirects from
old addresses are edited here too. Metafields add typed fields to products,
customers and orders (a care note, a material from a list), and those marked
for the storefront show in the product's specifications. Each store also has
product feeds for shopping engines, per market, and an Atom and RSS feed of its
blog; the online store page lists their addresses.

*Apps.* The store owner can place third-party scripts and widgets at named places
on the storefront. Each one says which cookie category it needs, and it loads
only for shoppers who accepted that category.

*Operations.* The report builder groups sales by up to two dimensions (day,
product, channel, source) and saves the result. The cohort grid shows how many
customers of each month came back, and the attribution page shows sales by the
campaign or site the customer came from. Permission sets give a staff member
access to only some areas, at one location or all of them. The inbox answers
chat messages live, with assignment and saved replies: a conversation page shows
the shopper typing and reading, and a call from the storefront rings anywhere in
the inbox and is answered on its conversation. The listings page imports a marketplace CSV
as draft products, combining the colors of one item into a single listing.

==== The register's extras

At the register, a card payment can offer tip presets. A discount past the
store's threshold, a price override or a refund asks a manager to enter their
PIN; the approval travels with the sale, so it also works offline.
A cart can be parked and resumed later with its customer, and an item not in the
catalog can be rung up by name and price. A layaway puts goods aside on a deposit
and hands them over when the last payment is made; cancelling refunds what was
paid less the store's fee. A completed sale can be edited, adding or removing
items with the difference charged or refunded, and shelf price labels can be
printed for any products.
