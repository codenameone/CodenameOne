== Commerce

Commerce is an optional service that validates in-app purchases and subscriptions server-side and gives your app a single, store-agnostic question to ask: _does this user have this entitlement right now?_ It answers that question for a customer on every platform at once, so a subscription bought on an iPhone unlocks the same account on Android, on the desktop and on the web. On top of entitlements it adds offerings you configure remotely, paywalls and a customer center you design in the console, targeting and experiments, lifecycle webhooks, integrations and revenue reports.

Commerce wraps the `Purchase` API (see the <<Monetization,monetization chapter>>) and doesn't replace it. Purchases still go through the platform store, and code that talks to `Purchase` directly keeps working unchanged. When the service can't answer -- commerce is switched off in the build, the device is offline, or the account is over its plan -- the app falls back to the store's own receipts, so a paying user keeps access.

Codename One doesn't process payments and never touches your money. The stores, or your own Stripe or Paddle account for web purchases, do. Commerce is infrastructure that sits beside the money flow.

==== Mapping products to entitlements

The *Products* page of the console holds the mapping. Each row names a store, a product id in that store and the entitlement the product grants. One entitlement usually has several products: a monthly and an annual subscription in each store, and perhaps a lifetime purchase. Changing the entitlement of a product moves the product's existing grants with it, so customers who already bought it follow the new mapping.

An entitlement is active when *any* of its granting products is active. A promotional grant and a paid subscription, or a sandbox and a production purchase, can both map to `pro` without one hiding the other. Access is decided by a single rule -- the latest expiry over the active grants, evaluated against the service's clock -- so a device clock set back can't extend it. Everything else (will-renew, billing stage, period type, family sharing) is advisory and never removes access by itself.

==== The stored answer and degraded mode

The last answer the service gave is stored on the device, per customer. After a cold start `isEntitled` and `getCustomerInfo` answer from it at once, before any network call and with no network at all. A stored entitlement is reported exactly as it was stored, even when its expiry date has passed by the device's clock. The device clock is the user's to set, so only the service decides when an entitlement ends, and it does so on the next `refresh()`.

`isDegraded()` is true after a call that couldn't get an answer the app can trust: the service was unreachable, the response failed its signature check, or the account is over the validated volume of its plan. While it's true, the stored answer stays in place, and `isEntitled` also accepts the store's own receipt, treating the entitlement id as a subscription product id. A paying user is therefore not locked out while the network is down.

When commerce isn't enabled in the build, or the manager is degraded, you can check the granting product yourself, as the receipt synchronization example does. Don't do it when the service answered no: a refunded or revoked receipt still looks active on the device, and treating that as a grant unlocks the app after the service denied it.

==== Transfer policies

When a signed-in user logs in to an account while the device's purchases already belong to _another_ known account, the transfer policy of the app decides who keeps them. Set it on the *App settings* page of the console:

[cols="2,5"]
|===
|Policy |Behavior

|`TRANSFER`
|The purchases move to the account that logged in last. This is the default, and it matches what the stores do when a device changes hands.

|`KEEP_WITH_ORIGINAL`
|The purchases stay with the account that made them. The new account gets nothing from this device's receipts.

|`TRANSFER_IF_NO_ACTIVE`
|The purchases move only when the account that holds them has no active entitlement of its own.
|===

Every move is reported to your backend as a `TRANSFER` webhook event.

=== Offerings and packages

An *offering* is the set of things you're selling at one moment: "the standard plans," "the holiday sale." A *package* is one entry of an offering -- a store product together with the role it plays, such as the monthly plan. Because offerings live in the console, you change what the app sells, and at what price points, without shipping an update.

==== Console setup

On the *Offerings* page, create an offering and add packages to it. Each package has:

- An id that's unique inside the offering.
- A type: `LIFETIME`, `ANNUAL`, `SIX_MONTH`, `THREE_MONTH`, `TWO_MONTH`, `MONTHLY`, `WEEKLY` or `CUSTOM`.
- A product id for each store the app ships to. The service hands each device the product of its own store.
- The billing period, and the free trial and introductory price if the product has them. Enter these as you configured them in the store. Periods are ISO-8601 durations such as `P1M` or `P7D`.

Mark one offering as the current one. That's the offering `getCurrent()` returns unless a targeting rule or an experiment selects another.

`getOfferings` is a blocking network call. The last answer is stored on the device and is what the call returns when the service can't be reached, so a paywall still opens offline. It never returns null. The result is empty, and `getCurrent()` is null, when commerce isn't enabled in the build or there has never been an answer.

After a fresh answer the manager asks the store for the products of the packages, where the platform lists products, and attaches each to its package. `getProduct()` then carries the store's localized price and name. On a platform whose store doesn't list products it stays null.

`Offering` has shortcuts for the standard types -- `getMonthly()`, `getAnnual()`, `getWeekly()` and `getLifetime()` -- and `getPackage(id)` for any other. `getMetadata()` returns the free-form values you attached to the offering in the console, which is a convenient place for a headline or a feature flag that should change with the offering.

==== Placements

A _placement_ is a named place in your app that sells something: `"onboarding"`, `"settings"`, `"export_limit"`. Pass it to `getOfferings(String)`. A targeting rule in the console can give a placement an offering of its own, so the upsell after onboarding and the one behind a locked feature can differ without a code change. A placement with no rule gets the current offering.

==== Trial and introductory eligibility

`isEligibleForIntro()` tells you whether this customer may still take the free trial or introductory price of a package. The service computes it from the customer's purchase history across platforms, so a customer who used a trial on one device isn't promised another one elsewhere. Use it to decide whether a paywall says "Start your free week" or "Subscribe." The built-in paywall variables described below follow the same rule.

=== Targeting and experiments

Targeting and experiments decide which offering `getCurrent()` returns for a given customer. Both are evaluated by the service on every `getOfferings` call, so they take effect without an app update.

==== Targeting rules

A targeting rule on the *Targeting* page names an offering and the conditions under which a customer gets it. A rule can match on:

- *Country* -- one or more store countries.
- *Platform* -- the platform the app is running on.
- *App version* -- a minimum version, a maximum version, or both.
- *Attribute* -- a subscriber attribute with a given value.
- *Placement* -- the placement the app asked for.

A condition you leave empty matches everyone. Rules are tried in priority order, lowest number first, and the first rule whose conditions all match wins. When no rule matches, the customer gets the current offering.

==== Experiments

An experiment splits customers between two or more offerings so that you can compare them. On the *Experiments* page, give the experiment an id, optionally limit it to a placement, and add variants. Each variant names an offering and a weight. Start the experiment when it's ready and stop it when you have an answer.

Assignment is deterministic. The service hashes the experiment id together with the app user id and maps the result onto the variant weights, so a customer always sees the same variant, on every device and after every restart, and nothing about the assignment needs to be stored. A customer who logs in moves from the anonymous id to the account id and is assigned by the account id from then on.

[cols="1,5"]
|===
|Path |What it does

|`MANAGE`
|Opens the store's own subscription management. On a platform with none, it opens the customer's management page, which is the billing portal for a subscription bought on the web.

|`REFUND`

==== Cancel reasons and retention offers

For the `CANCEL` path you define a list of reasons, such as "Too expensive" or "Not using it enough." The reason a customer picks is reported to the service, and the console charts the answers.

Each reason can carry a retention offer: a product, a line of text, and optionally the id of a promotional offer. A customer who picks that reason is shown the text and can accept or decline. Accepting buys the product, under the promotional offer when one is named and the store supports it. Declining continues to the store's subscription management, where the customer cancels.

A promotional offer is an App Store discount for existing or lapsed subscribers that the store sells only when it comes with a signature made by the developer's key. === Analytics and invites

Commerce reports what happens around a purchase to the analytics providers the app registered (see the <<analytics,analytics chapter>>), and it joins a customer to the invite that brought them in (see <<analytics-invites,Invite a friend>>). Neither needs code. An app with no provider sends nothing, and nothing is sent for a user whose consent doesn't cover analytics.

==== Segmenting by experiment

Because the paywall and purchase events carry `experiment` and `variant`, any analytics report can be split by variant: how many customers of each variant opened the paywall, picked the annual package, or bought. `experiment_exposure` is sent once for each experiment and variant an install is given, which gives every variant its denominator even for a customer who never opens a paywall.

==== Purchases by invited users

The service knows which install belongs to which customer, so a purchase by a customer who arrived through an invite is attributed to the inviter. The revenue it reports for that invite is verified: it's the amount of the purchase the service validated with the store, and not a value the app claimed. You don't call `Invites.conversion` for a purchase. Keep it for the conversions the service can't see, such as finishing a tutorial.

When the user logs in with `logIn`, the attribution follows the customer to the account. The app user id of commerce and the user id of analytics stay separate, and logging in here doesn't change the one you set with `Analytics.setUserId`.

When an invite is matched to the install, commerce asks the service for the customer again in the background, and the customer information listener hears about the reward. `getReferralRewards()` lists what was granted and why, for example to thank the user:

=== Stores

==== Apple App Store, on iOS and on the Mac

iOS purchases work with no setup beyond the receipt store. A native Mac build sells through the Mac App Store with the same code, and the service validates its receipts with the same Apple credentials. If the Mac app's bundle id differs from the package name of the project, enter it as the Mac bundle id on the *App settings* page of the console so that its receipts are accepted.

Point App Store Server Notifications V2 at `https://cloud.codenameone.com/api/v2/commerce/notifications/apple` in App Store Connect, so that renewals, cancellations, refunds and billing problems reach the service when they happen.

==== Google Play

Android builds sell through Google Play by default. In the Play Console, set up Real-time developer notifications and route the Pub/Sub topic to `https://cloud.codenameone.com/api/v2/commerce/notifications/google`. Google purchases are validated with Google's own server API, so the service account described under <<commerce-store-credentials,store credentials>> is required for them. Without it the service accepts the purchase in degraded mode and the app relies on the device's receipt.

==== Store credentials

By default the service validates the store-signed receipt the device presents. For Apple that's enough to gate features and it works with no setup. For _server-authoritative_ validation, where the service calls the stores' own server APIs to confirm the real subscription state, provide your credentials in the console. Apple and Google credentials are entered on the *Setup* page; Amazon, Stripe and Paddle credentials on the *Stores* page:

[cols="2,3,4"]
|===
|Store |Secrets |Used for

|Apple
|`commerce.apple.keyId`, `commerce.apple.issuerId`, `commerce.apple.privateKey` (the contents of the `.p8` file)
|Confirming subscription status for iOS and Mac purchases, recording renewals at their real amount, and signing promotional offers.

|Google
|`commerce.google.serviceAccountJson` (the full JSON key of a service account with Android Publisher access)
|Validating every Google Play purchase.

|Amazon
|`commerce.amazon.sharedSecret`
|Validating Amazon Appstore receipts.

|Stripe
|`commerce.stripe.secretKey`, `commerce.stripe.webhookSecret`
|Creating checkouts and portals, and verifying Stripe's notifications.

|Paddle
|`commerce.paddle.apiKey`, `commerce.paddle.notificationSecret`
|Creating checkouts and portals, and verifying Paddle's notifications.
|===

Secrets are stored AES-256-GCM encrypted. They're decrypted only server-side at call time and are never shown in the console again.

Store notifications are idempotent. Each is deduplicated on the id the store gives it, so a redelivery never double-counts, and a store transaction is counted once however many devices present its receipt.

=== Server side

==== Lifecycle webhooks to your backend

Commerce can forward every normalized state change to your own backend. Configure a URL and a signing secret on the *Webhook* page of the console. Each event is HMAC-SHA256 signed (header `CN1-Commerce-Signature`) and carries:

- A *per-customer monotonic `sequence`*, so your consumer can reject stale events and never has to reorder defensively.
- The grant's *`state_version`*. Reconcile against this.
- An always-present *`effective_expiration_at`*.
- The event `id` for idempotent deduplication.
- The *`event_type`*.

Delivery is at-least-once, in sequence order per customer, with retries that back off exponentially. The *Webhook* page lists recent deliveries and their outcome.

[cols="2,5"]
|===
|Event type |Sent when

|`INITIAL_PURCHASE`
|A customer buys a subscription for the first time.

|`RENEWAL`
|A subscription renews.

|`NON_RENEWING_PURCHASE`
|A one-time product is bought: a lifetime unlock or a consumable.

|`CANCELLATION`
|The customer turns renewal off. Access continues until the period ends.

|`UNCANCELLATION`
|The customer turns renewal back on.

|`EXPIRATION`
|A subscription ends.

|`BILLING_ISSUE`
|A renewal payment failed.

|`PRODUCT_CHANGE`
|The customer moved to another product of the same subscription group.

|`SUBSCRIPTION_PAUSED`
|The subscription was paused.

|`SUBSCRIPTION_EXTENDED`
|The store extended the renewal date.

|`REFUND`
|A purchase was refunded.

|`REFUND_REVERSED`
|A refund was reversed.

|`TRANSFER`
|Purchases moved from one app user id to another.

|`TEMPORARY_ENTITLEMENT_GRANT`
|Access was granted for a short time while a purchase couldn't be validated.

|`PROMOTIONAL_GRANT`
|An entitlement was granted from the console or the REST API.

|`PROMOTIONAL_REVOKE`
|Such a grant was revoked.
|===

Your backend can read and change customers through a REST API. Create a secret key on the *API keys* page. A key starts with `sk_`, belongs to one app, and is shown once when you create it, so store it then. Send it as a bearer token to `https://cloud.codenameone.com/api/v2/commerce/v1`:

[cols="1,4,5"]
|===
|Method |Path |What it does

|`GET`
|`/subscribers/{appUserId}`
|Returns the customer: `serverTime`, `appUserId`, `originalAppUserId`, `managementUrl`, `activeSubscriptions`, `entitlements`, `attributes` and `currencies`.

|`DELETE`
|`/subscribers/{appUserId}`
|Deletes the customer with their grants, attributes and currency history.

|`POST`
|`/subscribers/{appUserId}/attributes`
|Sets attributes. The body is `{"attributes": {"name": "value"}}`, and a null value deletes an attribute.

|`POST`
|`/subscribers/{appUserId}/entitlements/{entitlementId}/grant`
|Grants an entitlement. The optional body fields are `expiresDate`, in milliseconds since the epoch, and `reason`. Without an expiry the grant doesn't end.

|`POST`
|`/subscribers/{appUserId}/entitlements/{entitlementId}/revoke`
|Revokes a grant made this way. Purchases aren't affected.

|`POST`
|`/receipts`
|Validates a receipt your backend holds. The body names `appUserId`, `store` and `productId`, and carries the receipt as `signedTransaction` for Apple, `purchaseToken` for Google, or `signedTransaction` with `amazonUserId` for Amazon.

|`POST`
|`/receipts/import`
|Imports receipts in bulk from a CSV body.

|`GET`
|`/offerings`
|Lists the offerings of the app.

|`GET`
|`/subscribers/{appUserId}/currency`
|Returns the customer's virtual currency balances.

|`POST`
|`/subscribers/{appUserId}/currency`
|Changes a balance. The body is `code`, `delta`, and optionally `reason` and `reference`.
|===

A currency change with a negative `delta` spends. When the balance is too low the call answers `409` with the error `insufficient_balance` and changes nothing. `reference` makes the call safe to repeat: a second call with the same reference returns the first result, marked `replayed`, and doesn't change the balance again. Use your own order id as the reference.

An unknown customer answers `404`, and a request the service can't read answers `400`.

==== Granting entitlements from the console

The *Customers* page of the console has the same grant and revoke controls as the REST API. Pick an entitlement, optionally an expiry, and grant it. This is the way to give a customer access as a goodwill gesture, to unlock an account for a reviewer, or to give your team the paid features. A granted entitlement has the provenance `PROMOTIONAL`, is excluded from revenue, and reaches the app on its next `refresh()`.

==== Export and import

The *Export* page downloads two CSV files on demand, and can also deliver both every day to an Amazon S3 or Google Cloud Storage bucket you name.

[cols="1,5"]
|===
|File |Columns

|Transactions
|`createdAt`, `appUserId`, `store`, `environment`, `productId`, `eventType`, `transactionId`, `originalTransactionId`, `price`, `currency`, `usd`, `validationState`

|Subscribers
|`appUserId`, `entitlementId`, `active`, `productId`, `store`, `expiresDate`, `willRenew`, `provenance`

----

Each row is validated exactly as a receipt from a device is, so an imported subscription is checked with the store and continues to renew, expire and report events like any other. The answer lists the rows that weren't imported, with the reason for each.

==== The console

The *Commerce* section in the console at https://cloud.codenameone.com/console/index.html is account-level by default, with an app filter to narrow to one app. Beside the configuration pages this chapter has described, it reports on the business:

- *Overview* -- the subscription dashboard: headline KPIs (MRR, ARR, ARPU, active subscriptions, active trials, churn, trial conversion, customers) and trend charts for MRR, active subscriptions and revenue.
- *Insights* -- MRR movement (new versus churned), MRR forecast, realized-LTV curve, active subscriptions by store and by product, cohort retention, trials started, conversion to paying, refund rate over time and subscription status.
- *Customers* -- each app user with their active entitlements, attributes and balances.
- *Transactions* -- the recent validated-event ledger.

Commerce normalizes every active subscription to a monthly figure (MRR) and reconstructs the trends from the subscription history. Every chart pairs a graph with its underlying data table for export-friendly inspection. All money is shown in your reporting currency, and transactions in other currencies are normalized to USD through daily exchange rates.

=== Build hints

Commerce reuses the standard `build_key` that every Codename One cloud build already carries, so a cloud build needs no build hint to use the service. `CommerceManager.isCloudEnabled()` is true on any cloud build. These hints change the defaults:

[cols="2,1,4"]
|===
|Build hint |Default |Description

|`commerce.cloud.enabled`
|`true`
|Set to `false` to keep every decision on the device, from the store's own receipts. Every `CommerceManager` call then defers to the `Purchase` API.

|`commerce.cloud.endpoint`
|_(Codename One cloud)_
|The base URL of the commerce service, for example `\https://commerce.example.com/api/v2/commerce`. It must be an `https` URL. Plain `http` is accepted only for a service on a loopback address -- `localhost`, `127.0.0.1`, `[::1]`, or `10.0.2.2`, the host machine as the Android emulator sees it -- for testing on your own machine. A build with any other value fails.

|`commerce.cloud.publicKey`
|_(Codename One cloud)_
|The base64 encoded Ed25519 public key that responses of the service are verified against. A self-hosted service needs it: set it together with `commerce.cloud.endpoint`. While the app knows no public key, it uses no answer of the service.

|`android.billing.store`
|`play`
|The store an Android app is built for: `play` or `amazon`.
|===

==== Response signing and self-hosting

The answers that decide what a customer owns -- customer information, offerings, the customer center and promotional offer signatures -- are signed by the service with an Ed25519 key. Each request carries a fresh random nonce, and the signature covers that nonce together with the response. An answer recorded earlier can't be replayed to a later request, and an answer forged by something between the device and the service doesn't verify.

An answer is trusted only when it's signed and the signature verifies against the service's public key. An answer that's unsigned or wrongly signed is ignored, and so is every answer while the app knows no public key at all. The manager then becomes degraded and the app falls back to the stored answer and the store's receipts, exactly as it does when the network is down.

The stored answer is kept as the service signed it, and its signature is checked again every time the app starts. A stored answer that no longer verifies, for example after the key changed, is deleted rather than used.

=== Privacy

Commerce stores entitlement state and transaction metadata (store, product id, event type, amount, validation state) keyed by your app user id, together with the attributes you set. It never stores card numbers or payment credentials. Those stay with Apple, Google, Amazon, Stripe, Paddle or your processor. Family shared purchases grant access but are excluded from revenue. Deleting a customer through the REST API removes their grants, attributes and currency history.

=== Setup checklist

. Map your store products to entitlements on the *Products* page of the console, and create an offering with a package for each product you sell on the *Offerings* page.
. In the app, install a receipt store, call `refresh()` off the EDT on start, and gate features with `isEntitled(...)`.
. Show the offering with `Paywall.show(...)`, and add `CustomerCenter.show()` to your settings screen.
. Call `logIn(...)` when the user signs in to your account system and `logOut()` when they sign out.
. Point the stores' server notifications at the service, and enter your store credentials on the *Setup* and *Stores* pages.
. Optionally, set a webhook URL and secret to receive lifecycle events on your backend, and enable the integrations you use.
