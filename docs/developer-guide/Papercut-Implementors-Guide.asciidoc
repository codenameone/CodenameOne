[[papercut-implementors-guide]]
== Papercut: Making the arena shooter your own

Papercut is a twin-stick arena shooter drawn in cut paper and ink on a page of a
school notebook. You fly a paper quill, the pencil fires in the direction of your
right thumb, and folded paper creatures arrive from the edges of the page. Every
creature you cut up leaves a stain of its own ink behind, so a long run turns the
page into a record of everything that happened on it.

It shares the module layout and the accounts with <<skirmish-implementors-guide,Skirmish>>
and <<splat-implementors-guide,Splat>>, but it is deliberately smaller: a game that
runs entirely on the phone, and a server that keeps the scores honest without
playing the game itself. This chapter shows where each part lives and what to change
to make the game yours.

The game itself plays without a server; you only need one to sign in, keep scores
and shop. Four demo accounts exist on the development profile, each with the
password `papercut-demo`:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Account | What it's for

| `player@papercut.example`
| A player with runs on the boards.

| `rival@papercut.example`
| A second player to compare scores with.

| `trouble@papercut.example`
| A player with a flagged run, so the console has something to review.

| `admin@papercut.example`
| The administrator.
|===

A field of rivals with runs spread over all three modes, this week and today, is
seeded too, so every board and the daily sheet have something on them. Outside the
development profile only the admin is made, and only when the
`papercut.admin.email` and `papercut.admin.password` properties say who.

[[papercut-what]]
=== What the sample does

The home screen plays the game by itself behind the menu: a `Bot` flies an
`ArenaView` in Classic mode as an attract loop. The menu offers three ways to play:

Classic:: Survive. Creatures arrive in waves that grow harder, every one you cut
drops a drop of ink, and every drop you pick up raises the multiplier, up to x20.
Losing the quill resets the multiplier to x1.
Today's sheet:: Classic on the day's seed, so everyone who plays today faces the same
waves and competes on the same board.
Fold:: No pencil. Paper gates drift across the page, and you burst them by steering
the quill between their two ends.

The controls are a floating stick on each half of the screen: the left thumb moves
the quill, the right thumb aims and fires while it is held. The eraser, the game's
bomb, is a button at the top right that wipes every creature off the page. On a
desktop, WASD moves, the arrow keys aim and fire and Space erases; the mouse aims and
fires while its button is held; and a gamepad uses its two sticks and A or either
shoulder button.

[[papercut-modules]]
=== The modules

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Module | What's in it

| `shared`
| The API between the app and the server: the `com.codenameone.examples.papercut.api`
  interfaces (`AccountApi`, `RunApi`, `ShopApi`, `ProgressApi`, `FriendsApi`,
  `AdminApi`), their DTOs, and `ScoreBounds`, the arithmetic both sides agree a
  run cannot exceed.

| `common`
| The app. `play` is the game: `Arena`, `Sheet`, `Enemy`, `Shot`, `Drop` and `Bot`
  have no UI in them; `ArenaView`, `Hud`, `Art` and `Sfx` draw and sound it. `ui` has
  the forms, `net` the session, and `engage` the store, ads and notifications.

| `backend`
| The server: `account`, `run` (tickets, results, boards and the daily seed),
  `shop`, `progress`, `friends`, `push` and `admin`, over the entities in
  `domain`.

| `ios`
| The iOS build and its native settings.
|===

There is no separate rules module. The game lives in the app alone, because the
server never plays it again (see <<papercut-honest-scores>>).

[[papercut-arena]]
=== The arena runs without a screen

`Arena` is the whole game, as state and one `step(float)`. Positions are in sheet
units on an `Arena.WIDTH` by `Arena.HEIGHT` page, and whatever controls the quill
sets `moveX`, `moveY`, `aimX`, `aimY`, `firing` and `bombPressed` before each step.
What the player should see or hear happen -- a burst, a pick-up, a lost quill -- goes
to an `Arena.Listener`, which `ArenaView` implements and turns into paper scraps, ink
and sound.

That split is what lets `ArenaTest` play a hundred runs with the `Bot` and no
display, and it is what makes the attract loop on the home screen free: the bot sets
the same fields a thumb would.

The modes are the constants `Arena.CLASSIC`, `Arena.FOLD` and `Arena.DAILY`, passed
to the constructor with a seed. Classic and the daily share one director that picks a
creature kind by cost as the run goes on; Fold has its own that sets out the gates.
The multiplier climbs by one for every drop picked up and stops at
`Arena.MAX_MULTIPLIER`; the pencil gets more strokes as it climbs (`streams()`), which
is the reward for staying alive.

==== The creatures

Every creature is an `Enemy`, and the kinds differ only in how `Arena` steers them:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Kind | Behaviour

| `DART` | Folds toward the quill and keeps folding: the simplest chaser.
| `PINWHEEL` | Drifts and bounces, spinning; harmless alone, deadly in a crowd.
| `CRANE` | Glides in curves; cut it and it unfolds into two cranelets.
| `CRANELET` | What a crane leaves behind: small, fast, orbiting.
| `SNIPPER` | Lines up with the quill, then snips across the sheet in a straight dash.
| `INKWELL` | A pool of ink that pulls everything toward it, and bursts into darts when it has drunk too much.
| `GATE` | The gate of the Fold mode.
|===

Their size, points, hit points and colour are the arrays `Enemy.RADIUS`,
`Enemy.POINTS`, `Enemy.HP` and `Enemy.COLOUR`, indexed by kind. Adding a creature is
a new constant, a row in each array, a branch in the arena's steering and a shape in
the art script.

[[papercut-view]]
=== Drawing the page

`ArenaView` extends `com.codename1.gaming.GameView`, and draws everything as sprites
on the game layer: the page, the stains, the drops, the creatures, the shots and the
quill. Every cut-out is drawn twice, the second time tinted black and offset as its
shadow, so no image carries a shadow of its own. `Hud` paints the score, the
multiplier, the lives, the eraser button and the sticks over it.

The page is a `Sheet`: a grid of springs, one point mass for every crossing of the
ruled lines, each tied to where it was printed. A burst pushes the points out with
`poke(float, float, float, float)`, an inkwell pulls them in, and the springs bring
them back, so the lines ripple and pucker the way paper would if you could poke it.
The view draws the horizontal lines blue and one column red, which is all it takes to
read as a notebook. Like the arena, the sheet is plain arithmetic and runs in tests.

Every kill leaves an ink stain in the creature's colour. The stains are a ring
buffer, so a long run fills the page and the oldest marks give way to new ones
rather than slowing the game down.

==== Two sticks by hand

The gaming package's `TouchControls` offers one joystick, and a twin-stick shooter
needs two. `ArenaView` therefore reads the pointer itself: a thumb that lands on the
left half of the view becomes `ArenaView.move`, one on the right half `ArenaView.aim`,
and each `ArenaView.Stick` is centred wherever its thumb landed. The pointer callbacks
write the sticks on the EDT, and the render thread reads them when it copies the
controls into the arena. The eraser button is a circle the view and the `Hud` agree on
through `eraserButton()`.

[[papercut-honest-scores]]
=== Honest scores without a second copy of the game

Skirmish and Splat run the same deterministic rules on the phone and the server, so
the server can play a match again and know its outcome. Papercut does not. Keeping a
second copy of a real-time shooter on the server, in step with every change to the
game, costs more than a sample's leaderboards are worth, and it would force the game
to be bit-for-bit deterministic across devices.

Instead a result is held to two things:

* *A ticket.* Before a run, the app asks `RunApi` to start one, and `Runs.start`
  issues a single-use ticket for that mode. The result must answer a ticket this
  server issued, once, for the mode it was issued for. A daily ticket also pins the
  day's seed, so a daily result is always for the sheet everyone else was dealt.
* *Arithmetic.* `ScoreBounds` states what a run can honestly claim: the most a
  creature is worth, the multiplier's ceiling, the most kills a second and the
  longest run. `Runs.submit` refuses a result past those bounds, and keeps one that
  is merely remarkable but files a flag for the console.

[source,java]
----
include::../../scripts/papercut/shared/src/main/java/com/codenameone/examples/papercut/api/ScoreBounds.java[]
----

A cheat that stays under the bound gets a plausible score; that is the trade, and
`ScoreBounds` is the place to tighten it. `ArenaTest` plays the real game against
these numbers, so a change to the game that breaks them fails a test rather than a
leaderboard.

[[papercut-server]]
=== The server

Accounts and sign-in::
`account` registers players, signs them in and makes the demo accounts.
Boards::
`Boards.leaderboard` answers each player's best standing run of a mode over a period:
`ALL`, `WEEK`, `TODAY` or `FRIENDS` (`Modes`). A board is worked out from the runs on
every request rather than kept as a table of bests, because a week or a day is a
window a stored best cannot answer, and a struck run or a benched player has to vanish
from every board at once. A game with millions of runs would keep a per-period table.
The daily seed::
`DailySeeds.seedOf` is a hash of the UTC date unless an admin has picked a seed for
that day, so a day nobody played still has one and two servers agree without talking.
Friends::
A friendship goes one way: adding someone puts them on your `FRIENDS` board, not you
on theirs, so nobody has to accept anything and nobody can push onto a stranger's
board.
The shop::
`Catalog` lists the quills (paper colours bought with coins), spare erasers and two
coin packs sold through the platform stores. `Shop` takes the profile row's lock
before every change, so two taps on *Buy* cannot spend the same coins, and checks
store receipts through a `ReceiptValidator`; the development profile simulates them.
Rewarded ads credit coins once per nonce.
Achievements::
`Achievements` stores nothing. Each is worked out from the player's standing runs
whenever asked, so striking a run takes back what it unlocked, and a new achievement
counts every run played before it existed.
Administration::
`AdminApi` covers the stats, a player search, actions on a player, the flags and their
resolution, picking the daily seed, broadcasts and the audit log. `Moderation` writes
every action to the audit log in the same transaction as the change, so the log
cannot miss one.

[[papercut-art]]
=== The art pipeline

Nothing in Papercut is drawn by hand in an image editor. `tools/make-art.py` draws
the creatures, the quill, the ink drops, the eraser, the stains, the scraps and the
icon from polygons, then multiplies real photographed paper into every cut-out so it
reads as paper. The paper textures are CC0 from ambientCG, the sounds are CC0 packs
from OpenGameArt, and the two typefaces, Gochi Hand and Patrick Hand, are under the
SIL Open Font License. `ART.md` beside the project lists every source, its author and
its licence.

To make the art again, download the sources `ART.md` names into one directory and run
the script on it. It needs Pillow and ffmpeg, and rewrites every image and sound in
`common/src/main/resources` and the app icon:

[[papercut-reskin]]
=== Re-skinning

* *Colours.* A creature's colour is `PALETTE` at the top of the script and
  `Enemy.COLOUR` in the game, which its bursts and stains take. Change both together.
* *Shapes and sizes.* A creature's shape is one function in the script; its size on
  the page is `Enemy.RADIUS`.
* *The page.* Swap the paper textures for another surface -- graph paper, a
  blueprint, a chalkboard -- and change the line colours `ArenaView` draws the sheet
  with.
* *Tinted images.* Stains, scraps, pencil strokes and rings are drawn white on
  purpose, because the game tints them at run time.
* *Type and theme.* The fonts load through `Art.title(float)` and `Art.body(float)`,
  and the forms are styled by the theme in `common/src/main/css`.
