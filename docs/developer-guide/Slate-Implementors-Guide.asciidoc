[[slate-implementors-guide]]
== Slate: Building on the sample

Slate shares its shape with <<pulse-implementors-guide,Pulse>>,
<<savor-implementors-guide,Savor>> and <<wayline-implementors-guide,Wayline>>:
the module layout, accounts and sign-in, cards and the payment provider, text
messages, push notifications, the live channel and hosting the app in a browser.
This chapter covers what an appointment business changes: a slot engine that
asks every party at once, rules about gaps, and calendars that are read and
written in both directions.

=== The modules

`shared` holds the data classes and the `@RestClient` interfaces both sides build
from. `common` is the app, for every platform. `backend` is the server, in
packages by subject:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Package | What it holds

| `account`
| Accounts, phone verification, preferences and the demo accounts.

| `catalog`
| The business, its locations, services, providers, resources and verticals.

| `booking`
| The slot engine, availability, holds, bookings, rescheduling, the waitlist and
  the demo business.

| `provider`
| Working hours, time off and a provider's schedule.

| `sync`
| Connected calendars: Google, Microsoft, published ICS addresses, the phone's
  own calendars and the feed Slate publishes.

| `owner`
| The dashboard and the board of the day.

| `pay`
| Deposits and no-show fees: simulated or Stripe.

| `live`, `push`, `sms`
| The WebSocket, push notifications and text messages.
|===

Roles are `CLIENT`, `PROVIDER` and `OWNER`, in `Roles`. Every account is a
client; the others are granted by an owner, and the app offers a mode for each.

=== The slot engine

`SlotEngine` decides which starts can be booked. A start is offered only when
every party the service needs is free -- the providers, one resource of the
right kind, and the client themself -- and every rule the business set holds:
notice and horizon, buffers before and after, a provider's daily cap and breaks,
travel time between locations, the client's gap between bookings and their
bookings per day, and the shortest gap worth leaving on a provider's day. It reads

* *Your vertical.* A row in `Verticals` and, for the demo, a set of services.
* *Your brand.* Colors and shapes are in `common/src/main/css/theme.css`, text in
  `common/src/main/l10n`.
* *What it leaves out.* Slate books single appointments. Packages and memberships,
  gift cards, reviews, recurring appointments, intake forms beyond the one question
  a service can ask, tips and staff commission aren't included; each is a table
  and a screen on top of the booking rules, which stay as they are.
* *Your rules.* Every gap rule is a column of the business that the owner edits
  and previews; the engine is where a new rule goes, with a reason to report.

[[slate-before-product]]
=== Before it's a product

The sample is complete as a demonstration. A product needs at least:

* *Recurring events in ICS calendars.* A published calendar's repeating events
  (`RRULE`) aren't expanded; only single events count as busy. Google and
  Microsoft expand them on their side, so those are right.
* *Encrypted calendar credentials.* Refresh tokens and ICS addresses are stored as
  they are. Encrypt them at rest, with a key outside the database, and let a
  provider revoke them.
* *Safe fetching of calendar addresses.* The server fetches whatever ICS address a
  provider types. Restrict it to public `https` addresses, refuse private and
  loopback networks, and cap the size and time of each fetch.
* *Stricter concurrency on the daily rules.* Cells make a double booking
  impossible, but a provider's daily cap, their breaks and travel time, and a
  client's bookings per day are checked by the engine before the claim. Two
  bookings placed in the same instant for different times can both pass. The
  hold narrows the window; a product that must never exceed them takes a lock
  per provider and client day.
* *Tested late cancellation and no-show fees.* Taking a deposit is simulated and
  hasn't been tested against a real processor's capture and refund flows.
* *A real payment processor and PCI scope.* Wire `PaymentProvider` to your
  processor and test it with its test keys. Keep card entry on the processor's
  hosted page, confirm your PCI DSS self-assessment, and add receipts, tax,
  refunds and disputes.
* *Reviewed translations.* The non-English bundles are machine translations; have
  them reviewed before release.
* *Operations.* Monitoring, backups that have been restored in a test, a support
  process, and a channel between server instances before you run more than one.
