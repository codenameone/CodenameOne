== Pulse: Building on the sample

Pulse shares its shape with <<savor-implementors-guide,Savor>> and
<<wayline-implementors-guide,Wayline>>: the module layout, accounts and sign-in,
cards and the payment provider, text messages, push notifications, the live
channel and hosting the app in a browser. This chapter covers what a studio
changes: households, a timetable that never oversells, a door pass, recurring
billing, and the marketing that runs by itself.

=== The modules

`shared` holds the data classes and the `@RestClient` interfaces both sides build
from, so a change one side didn't follow is a compile error. `common` is the app,
for every platform. `backend` is the server, in packages by subject:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Package | What it holds

| `account`
| Accounts, phone verification, preferences and quiet hours, moderation, and the
  demo accounts.

| `studio`
| Locations, the timetable, households, bookings and the waitlist, door passes,
  and the demo studio.

| `desk`
| Check-in at the desk and the kiosk, member lookup, walk-ins and the desk's day.

| `trainer`
| Rosters, attendance, shifts and live heart rates.

| `billing`
| Memberships, invoices, proration, freezes, dunning and referral credits.

| `pay`
| The payment provider: simulated or Stripe.

| `engagement`
| Visit statistics, workouts, away periods and the automatic nudges.

| `social`
| Friends, the feed, kudos and class invites.

| `owner`
| The dashboard, plans, the timetable, staff and campaigns.

| `live`, `push`, `sms`
| The WebSocket, push notifications and text messages.
|===

Roles are `MEMBER`, `TRAINER`, `DESK` and `OWNER`, in `Roles`. Every account is a
member; the others are granted by an owner. The kiosk is a desk account on a
tablet, so it needs no role of its own.

=== A class that never oversells

The rule that must never break is that a class isn't booked past its capacity,
however many people tap Book at once. `Bookings` leaves it to the database: the
places taken are a column of the class, and the only statement that raises it does
so on condition that it's still below capacity. A booking whose statement changed
no row joins the waitlist instead. Two requests for the last place both run that
statement, and the database makes the second find the class full.

Everything else is checked first: the class is still to come, the person fits its
age range on the day, the waiver is signed, and a membership covers it. A place
given back promotes the first person on the waitlist, one promotion at a time per
class, and a job settles finished classes, turning the absent into no-shows.

The same pattern takes a trainer's offered shift: two trainers tapping Take
together are told apart by one conditional statement.

=== The door pass

A pass on a phone is a QR code reading `pulse:<member id>:<code>`, where the code
is an HOTP (RFC 4226, SHA-256, eight digits) of the pass's secret over the number
of 30-second periods since the epoch. The phone and the server make the same
`com.codename1.security.Otp` call, so the phone needs no signal. The server takes
a code one period either side of now, for a phone whose clock is slightly off, and
keeps every code it accepted until it could no longer be valid, so a screenshot
shown twice is refused as a replay.

An NFC id is the other kind of pass, for a fob or wristband. It doesn't change, so
it's as good as the thing it's written on; replacing the pass's secret replaces it
too.

A check-in always answers, admitted or refused with a reason, and never fails for
a member who simply isn't allowed in. The kiosk turns that answer into a green or
a red screen. Each check-in is also sent to staff over the live channel.

=== Billing

`Billing` charges in three steps and only the middle one talks to the processor:
a transaction writes the invoice and claims it, the card is charged with no
transaction open, and a second transaction records the result. The invoice id is
the processor's idempotency key, so a server that dies between the charge and the
record charges the same invoice again on the next run and is answered the first
charge, not a second one.

* *Proration.* A member moving plans has what's left of the old period taken off
  the first invoice of the new one.
* *Freezes.* One week to three months.
* *The dunning ladder.* A declined renewal is retried after one, three and seven
  days. From the second failure the membership is past due and the door says so;
  after the last, the invoice waits to be paid by hand.
* *Referral credits.* A member whose referral first pays gets a credit.
* *Amounts* are `long` minor units in `pulse.currency`.

The processor is behind `PaymentProvider`: a customer, a card saved to it once as
a token, and charges against that token with nobody present. The server keeps the
token, a brand and four digits, never a card number. `SimulatedPayments` takes the
test cards; `StripePayments` adds cards on Stripe's hosted page. Another processor
is one more implementation of the interface, chosen in `Services`.

=== Nudges and campaigns

The nudge job in `engagement` runs every 15 minutes and sends two kinds of message on its own: a class
starting in 3 to 36 hours that's under 60% full is offered to members who took
that kind of class before, at most three per empty place; and a member who hasn't
been in for 14 days is told the studio misses them, at most every 21 days.
Neither reaches a member who is away, one who turned promotions off, or one inside
their quiet hours; those are left for a later run. Each message is its own
transaction, checked again inside it, so two servers running the job send it once.

Campaigns are the owner's: a message to those who took a kind of class, every
active member, or the lapsed, counted as sent, opened, and booked.

=== Live heart rates

A member's watch sends heart rate readings during a class. `HeartRates` keeps the
latest per member in memory only: a reading matters for a few seconds, and the
workout, which is kept, arrives whole when it ends. The zone, one to five, is the
reading's share of 220 less the member's age, in fifths from 50%. Readings go to
the class's trainer over the live channel.

The live channel carries four frames: a class's places changing, one of an
account's bookings changing, a heart rate, and a check-in. The connections are
those of one process; running more than one instance needs a channel between them,
such as a database `LISTEN`/`NOTIFY` or a message broker, feeding the same
methods.

=== Settings

`backend/application.properties` lists every setting. A deployment sets the
database (`DATABASE_URL`), the issuer and signing key, the first owner
(`pulse.admin.email`, `pulse.admin.password`), and the providers:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Setting | Choices

| `pulse.sms.provider`
| `twilio`, `vonage`, `messagebird`, or `console` on a development profile.

| `pulse.payments.provider`
| `stripe`, or `simulated` on a development profile.

| `pulse.push.provider`
| `cloud` (with `pulse.push.app` and `pulse.push.key`), or `console` and
  `recording` on development profiles.

| `pulse.currency`, `pulse.timezone`
| The studio's currency and the time zone quiet hours are counted in.
|===

Unset, each provider is the development one on a development profile and the real
one elsewhere. A real provider with missing settings is logged at start-up, naming
them, and every call to it fails with the same message; nothing falls back to a
development stand-in. Secrets go in the environment, in upper case with `_` for
each dot (`PULSE_PAYMENTS_STRIPE_SECRET`).

=== Making it yours

* *Your studio.* Replace `DemoStudio` with your locations, rooms, class types and
  plans, or create them from the owner's screens on an empty database.
* *Your brand.* Colors and shapes are in `common/src/main/css/theme.css`, text in
  `common/src/main/l10n`.
* *Your rules.* The late-cancellation window, the dunning ladder, freeze limits,
  the referral credit and the nudge thresholds are constants beside the code that
  uses them, in `Bookings`, `Billing` and the nudge job in `engagement`.
* *A chain of studios.* Locations are already a dimension of the timetable and the
  desk; a chain adds location-scoped staff roles and reporting.

[[pulse-before-product]]
=== Before it's a product

The sample is complete as a demonstration and stops where a studio's legal and
physical systems begin. A product needs at least:

* *A real payment processor and PCI scope.* Wire `PaymentProvider` to your
  processor and test it with its test keys. Keep card entry on the processor's
  hosted page so card numbers never reach your app or server, and confirm your PCI
  DSS self-assessment with the processor. Add receipts, tax, refunds and the
  disputes process your market expects.
* *Waivers and medical consent that hold up.* The waiver is a flag and a date. A
  binding waiver needs the text your lawyer approves for your jurisdiction, a
  record of exactly which version was signed, by whom and how, and a health
  questionnaire or medical consent where the law or your insurer asks for one.
  Minors usually need a parent or guardian to consent for them.
* *Child safeguarding.* Children's classes need background checks for the staff who
  teach them, rules on who may collect a child, consent for photos, and limits on
  what other members can see about a child. Check what your country's safeguarding
  rules require.
* *Door hardware.* The kiosk decides; it doesn't open anything. A real door needs
  an access controller and reader, integrated through their vendor's API, with a
  fail-safe for fire exits and a plan for when the network is down.
* *Health data privacy.* Heart rates and workouts are health data. Under GDPR
  that's special category data, needing explicit consent, a stated purpose, a
  retention limit and deletion on request. In the United States, check whether
  your service falls under HIPAA (for example through a healthcare partner) and
  what state privacy laws ask. Follow Apple's and Google's rules for data read from
  HealthKit and Health Connect.
* *Marketing consent.* Nudges and campaigns respect the promotion switch and quiet
  hours. Check your market's rules on marketing messages, such as opt-in
  requirements and an unsubscribe link in every message.
* *Reviewed translations.* The non-English bundles are machine translations; have
  them reviewed before release.
* *Operations.* Monitoring, backups that have been restored in a test, a support
  process for members, and a channel between server instances before you run more
  than one.
