[[savor-implementors-guide]]
== Savor: Building on the sample

Savor began as a copy of <<wayline-implementors-guide,Wayline>> and kept most of
what isn't about rides: the module layout, the accounts and sign-in, the cards and
the payment provider, text messages, push notifications, the live channel, the map,
the operations console and hosting the app in a browser. This chapter covers what a
food-delivery service changes. For the parts the two share, it points at the Wayline
chapter, which is true of Savor with the names swapped.

[[savor-launch]]
=== Running it

The project is in `scripts/savor`, and the Initializr generates it under your own
package and name. Start the server on the development profile, which seeds the demo
accounts and restaurants and simulates the cards, the kitchens and a courier:

[source,bash]
----
CN1_PROFILE=dev backend/server.sh run
----

Then run the app in the simulator, from the project's directory:

[source,bash]
----
mvn verify -Psimulator -DskipTests -Dcodename1.platform=javase
----

`backend/server.sh run --web` builds the app for a browser as well and serves it at
`http://localhost:8080/`. Running on a device, and the compiled server, work as
<<wayline-launch>> describes.

=== The modules

The three modules are Wayline's: `shared` holds the contract, the data transfer
objects and the `@RestClient` interfaces both sides compile against; `common` is
the app; `backend` is the server. The backend's packages follow the domain:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Package | What it holds

| `catalog`
| Restaurants, their menus and photographs, the search that lists the restaurants
  that deliver to a point, and applications to list a restaurant.

| `order`
| Carts priced into quotes, orders and their state machine, promo codes, saved
  addresses, the order history and the simulated kitchen.

| `delivery`
| Deliveries and their state machine, offers to couriers and the simulated
  courier.

| `courier`, `onboarding`
| Couriers' shifts and earnings, and applications to deliver with their documents.
  Wayline's driver code under new names.

| `pay`, `sms`, `push`, `live`, `account`, `geo`, `admin`
| Taken from Wayline and adapted where an order differs from a ride.
|===

=== An order and its delivery

A ride is one thing that moves from requested to finished. An order is two. The
order is what the customer bought and the kitchen cooks, the delivery is what a
courier carries, and each has its own states:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Order | Delivery

| `PLACED`, then `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED` by the kitchen, `READY`, `PICKED_UP`,
  `DELIVERED`; or cancelled by the customer or an administrator, or `NO_COURIERS`
| `REQUESTED`, `OFFERED` to a courier, `ACCEPTED`, `ARRIVED` at the restaurant,
  `IN_PROGRESS` to the door, `COMPLETED`; or cancelled, or `NO_COURIERS`
|===

The delivery is Wayline's ride, almost unchanged: the pickup is the restaurant and
the drop-off is the door. Keeping it so let the whole courier side -- offers,
shifts, the moving marker, earnings, the guards on two answers at once -- come
across without being rewritten.

The delivery shares the order's id, so either side can find the other without a
join table. From then on the couriers are asked while the kitchen cooks, and the
order follows its delivery: the courier collecting the food moves the order to
`PICKED_UP`, and only from `READY`, so nobody leaves with an order the kitchen has
not finished; the delivery completing moves the order to `DELIVERED`. A delivery
that finds no courier ends the order with `NO_COURIERS` and lets the hold go.

Every move of either state is a conditional update that names the states it may
come from, so two answers at the same moment can't both win -- a customer
cancelling while the kitchen accepts, or two couriers taking one offer. It's the

=== Hold now, charge on arrival

A rider pays at the end of a ride, for a fare known by then. A customer pays for
food before anyone cooks it, but shouldn't be charged for an order that never
arrives. The card is therefore authorized for the total when the order is placed and
captured when it's delivered:

[source,java]
----
include::../../scripts/savor/backend/src/main/java/com/codenameone/examples/savor/pay/Payments.java[tag=savor-authorize,indent=0]
----

The provider interface Wayline's billing chapter describes gained the two calls
that make it possible, `authorize` and `capture`, and a `release` for a hold that
is let go:

[source,java]
----
include::../../scripts/savor/backend/src/main/java/com/codenameone/examples/savor/pay/PaymentProvider.java[tag=savor-payment-provider,indent=0]
----

* **Placed:** `Payments.authorize` holds the total. A refused card answers 402 and
  nothing is written. An order that couldn't be written lets its hold go at once.
* **Rejected or cancelled:** `Payments.release` lets the hold go. Nothing was taken,
  so there is nothing to refund.
* **Delivered:** `Payments.settle` captures the hold. A capture can still fail -- a
  hold expires, or a bank declines then -- and the receipt then says so, for an
  administrator to follow up.
* **After delivery:** a tip is a charge of its own; a refund, full or partial, is
  made against the captured payment.

The receipt is kept with the delivery. Before a delivery exists, a customer's
receipt is built from the order itself and shows the hold.

With Stripe, a hold is a PaymentIntent created with manual capture. Card holds
expire after about seven days, far longer than any meal takes. Turning Stripe on,
and the hosted page that keeps a card number away from the server, are exactly as
<<wayline-billing>> describes.

=== Prices

`order/Quotes` prices a cart, and does so on the server alone: the app shows the
quote it's given and sends only the dishes, their choices, the promo code and the
tip. The total is:

* The food, from the menu as it's when the order is placed, with each dish's
  choices checked against its groups' minimum and maximum.
* The delivery fee, a base plus a price per kilometer by road from the restaurant,
  times a surge an administrator can raise.
* A service fee, a percentage of the food.
* A flat small-order fee for food under a threshold.
* Tax, a percentage of the food after the discount.
* A promo code's discount, once per account.
* The tip.

An administrator sets the fees, the commission and the tax under **Pricing**. The
commission is what the service keeps of the food; the merchant's sales show the rest.

under `/api/merchant/restaurants/{id}/...`, and each request checks that the account

orders. The rules are in the same table as the rest of the server's:

The merchant's screen is built on the operations console's `Console`, so it's a
list beside a detail on a wide screen and a list that opens a screen per order on a
phone. A new order reaches it as a frame on the live channel, which makes it read
the queue again: the frames say what changed, never the data, as in Wayline.

Menu photographs are uploaded the way couriers' documents are, stored by the server
and served from it, so the seed data carries its own photographs rather than
pointing at someone else's site.

=== Simulated kitchens and couriers

A development server simulates the two people a customer would otherwise wait for.
`order/DemoKitchen` accepts, cooks and readies the orders of each restaurant that has
no merchant, and `delivery/DemoFleet` takes deliveries while no real courier
is online. Both are on only for the development profile, the way the simulated cards
are: the test profile turns them off, because the tests play the kitchen and the
courier themselves. A restaurant with a merchant is left to its staff, and the
simulated courier stands aside while a real one is online.

=== Tests

[source,bash]
----
mvn -pl shared,backend -Dcodename1.platform=backend test
mvn -pl shared,backend -Dcodename1.platform=backend test -Dcn1.backend.compiledTests=true
./run-e2e.sh
./run-web-e2e.sh
----

The backend tests run the server on the JVM and then compiled, against the order
and delivery state machines, the holds and captures, the quotes and promo codes and
who may call what. `run-e2e.sh` starts a server on the test profile and drives the
app in the simulator through every role -- an order placed, accepted, collected and
delivered, a card held and charged, a tip and a refund -- taking the screenshots in
the user's guide. `run-web-e2e.sh` does the same for the app in a browser.

=== Making it yours

The ways to make Wayline your own carry over: the name, icon and colors, the
languages, the text-message and push providers, the database, hosting. A few apply
to food in particular:

* **One restaurant or a small chain.** Ordering and delivery under the restaurant's
  own name, which <<savor-own-restaurant>> describes.
* **Pickup.** An order with no delivery is one the kitchen hands over itself. Mark
  the order as collected, skip `dispatch` when the kitchen accepts it, and let the
  merchant move it from `READY` to `DELIVERED`, which captures the hold.
* **Scheduled orders.** Store the time the customer wants the food, and accept the
  order for the kitchen only when its preparation time before that's reached.
* **Before it's a product.** A real service needs restaurant payouts, tax rules for
  the places it sells in, menus that change by the hour, allergens and age checks.
  None of them are in the sample.

[[savor-own-restaurant]]
=== Making it yours: A restaurant's own delivery service

A marketplace takes a share of every order and keeps the customer. A restaurant,
or a chain of a few, that runs its own ordering and delivery keeps both: the app
carries its name, the customers' accounts and order history are its own, and the
couriers are its staff or a courier company it contracts. Savor is most of that
service already. What changes is that there is one brand instead of many, and
nobody applies to sell. This section says what to change, and the class each
change belongs in.

==== One brand, one or a few kitchens

A chain is a restaurant row per branch, each with its own address, hours,
preparation time and menu. Seed them in place of `catalog/DemoRestaurants`, or
create them through the admin console. Nothing else in the catalog changes:
`Catalog.nearby` already answers the branches that deliver to the customer's
door, nearest first, and the order goes to the branch the customer picked.

The customer's first screen changes. With one kitchen, open its menu instead of
the list: where `Nav.build` makes the customer's home, call
`RestaurantForm.show` with the restaurant's id. With a few, keep the list, which
is now a list of branches, and drop the row of cuisines and the sort above it
in `CustomerForm`, which only make sense for many restaurants.

The name, the icon and the colors are the ones <<savor-launch>> and Wayline's
chapter describe. Use the restaurant's own photographs: replace the files in
`backend/photos`, which the development server reads into the database the first
time it starts, and name them in the seed. In production, upload them from the
merchant's menu editor.

==== Nobody applies to sell

Restaurants no longer join, so close that door:

* Remove the restaurant applications from the server's rules and from
  `Restaurants`: `apply`, `application` and `applicationPhoto`. The admin's
  **Applications** pane then lists couriers only.
* Set the commission to zero under **Pricing**, or with
  `savor.fee.commissionPercent=0`. The commission is what a marketplace keeps of
  the food; a restaurant selling its own food keeps it all, and the merchant's
  sales should say so.
* Make each branch's staff merchants of their branch from the admin console. A
  merchant account works on one restaurant, the one `Restaurants.mine` finds.
  An owner who watches several branches needs a branch picker on
  `MerchantForm` and the restaurant's id in the merchant's calls in place of
  `mine`.

==== Couriers of its own

A restaurant's couriers are known people, so the courier side works unchanged,
with the application step done by an admin. Approve couriers from
**Applications**, or give the role straight from **People**, and remove
**Deliver with Savor** from the customer's menu in `CustomerForm`, and the
courier choice from `RegisterForm`, if the public shouldn't apply.
Offers go to the nearest courier online, as before. With two or three couriers,
lengthen `savor.offer.seconds` and `savor.search.seconds`, because the one
courier nearby may be at a door.

Many restaurants deliver only part of the time, or hand deliveries to a courier
company at busy hours. `Deliveries.dispatch` is the one place a delivery
starts: send it to the company's API there instead of offering it to couriers,
and move the delivery with the updates the company sends back.

==== Pickup

A customer who collects the order needs no courier. Mark the order as collected
at checkout, skip `dispatch` when the kitchen accepts it in `Orders.accept`,
and let the merchant move it from `READY` to `DELIVERED`, which captures the
hold the way a delivery does.

==== Booking a table

A restaurant that delivers usually takes bookings too, and the parts are all in
the sample. A booking isn't in it, so this is the outline:

* **The record.** A `Reservation` entity -- restaurant, account, time, party
  size, a note and a state of requested, confirmed, declined, seated or
  cancelled -- in a new migration, with a state machine of conditional moves
  like the order's, so a guest cancelling while the restaurant confirms can't
  both win.
* **The contract.** A `@RestClient` interface in `shared` for the guest's calls
  (the free times, book, cancel, their own bookings) and calls under
  `/api/merchant/restaurants/{id}/...` for the staff, behind the same rules as
  the merchant's other calls.
* **Free times.** Slots inside the restaurant's opening hours, each with the
  number of covers the room takes, less the bookings already confirmed.
* **The staff's screen.** A tab beside the order queue in `MerchantForm`,
  refreshed by a frame on the live channel when a booking arrives, as a new
  order is.
* **Telling the guest.** A text message through `SmsSender` and a push through
  the `push` package when a booking is confirmed, and a reminder before it.

A deposit for a large party is a card hold, as an order is: authorize when the
booking is made, capture it for a no-show, and release it when the guest
arrives.
